# Three truths, two lies

## Goal

The aim of the activity is to write **five facts about yourself**:

- three true statements;
- two false statements.

Your classmates ask questions and try to identify the two lies.

## 1. Talking about yourself

You can write about:

- your summer holidays;
- your origins and where you live;
- your age;
- your family;
- your hobbies and passions;
- personal anecdotes.

## 2. Using *be* and *have*

### Use *be* for age and descriptions

| French idea | Correct English |
|---|---|
| J'ai quinze ans. | I **am** fifteen years old. |
| Je suis français. | I **am** French. |
| Je suis sportif. | I **am** athletic. |

!!! warning "Common mistake"
    Do not say **I have fifteen years old**. In English, age is expressed with the verb *be*: **I am fifteen years old**.

### Use *have* for possession and family

- I **have** a sister.
- I **have** two cats.
- I **have** a large family.

After a number greater than one, remember the plural: **two cats**, not **two cat**.

## 3. Talking about hobbies

Use the present simple to talk about regular activities and preferences.

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| activité régulière | I swim every week. |
| loisir | I go swimming. |
| préférence | I like swimming. |
| capacité | I can swim. |

!!! warning "Common mistake"
    To describe a usual activity, say **I swim** or **I go swimming**, not **I do swim**. The form *I do swim* is only used for emphasis or contradiction.

Other useful patterns:

- I play football.
- I play the guitar.
- I enjoy reading.
- I love video games.
- I like lemons.
- My favourite activity is drawing.

## 4. Building simple statements

### Affirmative form

With most verbs:

> Subject + verb + complement

- I live in Lyon.
- I speak two languages.
- I practise judo.

With *be*:

> Subject + am/is/are + complement

- I am fifteen years old.
- My parents are from Italy.

### Negative form

With most verbs:

> Subject + do not + base verb + complement

- I do not like football.
- I do not have a pet.

With *be*:

- I am not sixteen.
- My family is not from London.

## 5. Asking follow-up questions

Questions help you investigate and discover the lies.

| Statement | Possible question |
|---|---|
| I have two cats. | What are their names? |
| I swim every week. | Where do you swim? |
| I went to Spain this summer. | Who did you go with? |
| I have a sister. | How old is she? |
| I like lemons. | What is your favourite dessert? |

Useful question words:

- **Who?** for a person;
- **Where?** for a place;
- **When?** for a moment;
- **Why?** for a reason;
- **How?** for a manner;
- **How many?** for a number.

## 6. Guessing the lies

Use these expressions to give your opinion:

- I think this statement is true.
- I think number two is a lie.
- I do not believe you!
- This story sounds true.
- Are you sure?
- I guessed correctly.

## 7. Correcting the worksheet sentences

| Original attempt | Correct or more natural sentence |
|---|---|
| I have fifteen years old. | I am fifteen years old. |
| I have two cat. | I have two cats. |
| I do swim. | I swim. / I go swimming. |
| I have a sister. | I have a sister. |
| I like lemon. | I like lemons. |

## Model activity

1. I have two brothers.
2. I can play the piano.
3. I went to Canada last summer.
4. I do not like chocolate.
5. I speak three languages.

The reader must decide which three statements are true and which two are lies.

## Remember

- Use **be** to give your age: *I am fifteen years old*.
- Use **have** to talk about possession and family.
- Add **-s** to a plural noun after *two*, *three*, or another number greater than one.
- Use the present simple for habits and preferences.
- Ask precise follow-up questions to test whether a statement is believable.
